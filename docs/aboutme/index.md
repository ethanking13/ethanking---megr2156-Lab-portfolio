# About Me

I’ve always been interested in understanding how things work and figuring out how to make them better. That interest eventually led me to mechanical engineering, where I can combine problem-solving with the hands-on side of designing and building things. I’m especially interested in motorsports because it brings those ideas together. A vehicle is a system made up of hundreds of individual decisions, and small changes can have a noticeable effect on how everything works together.

One of the experiences that had the biggest impact on me was working on my own project car. It taught me that engineering does not always go exactly as planned. I had to work through constraints, make decisions with the resources I had, and figure out solutions when something did not work the first time. More importantly, it made me enjoy the process of troubleshooting and improving something piece by piece. Seeing a project develop from an idea into something I could actually work on gave me a better appreciation for engineering beyond what I learn in a classroom.

That same mindset has carried into my engineering projects at UNC Charlotte. I’ve worked with tools such as Creo, Python, and engineering calculations while developing projects that require both technical thinking and practical problem-solving. I’ve also learned that being an engineer is not just about finding an answer. It is about understanding the constraints behind a problem, explaining why a decision was made, and being willing to change your approach when the first solution is not the right one.

I’m still developing as an engineer, particularly in areas like CAD and design, but that is part of what motivates me. I want to continue building my technical skills while gaining more experience with real engineering problems. My goal is to become an engineer who can take an idea, understand the requirements and limitations, and turn it into something that works.
