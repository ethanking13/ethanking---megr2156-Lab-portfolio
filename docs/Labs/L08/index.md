# Problem Statement

### 1.1 Purpose of the Design

The purpose of this project is to design and manufacture a child-friendly toy flathead screwdriver for children ages 3–5. The screwdriver features a large, blocky design that makes it easier for young children to grip and handle. The goal is to encourage imaginative play, introduce children to basic tools, and help develop hand-eye coordination and motor skills.

The inspiration for this project came from my two younger siblings, who have several different playsets. Seeing them enjoy pretend activities inspired me to create a toy screwdriver that could be incorporated into their play.

### 1.2 How I Noticed the Problem

I noticed that children enjoy interactive toys that allow them to imitate everyday activities. This inspired me to create a toy screwdriver that gives young children another way to participate in pretend building and repair activities.

### 1.3 Ideal User

The ideal users are children between the ages of 3 and 5 who enjoy playing with building sets, tool sets, and other interactive toys. The product is intended for young children who are developing their coordination and learning through imaginative play.

### 1.4 Design Approach

My approach is to design the toy screwdriver using CAD software and then manufacture it using a 3D printer. I plan to create an oversized, blocky design that is easy for young children to grip. I also want to use a material that is strong enough to withstand regular play while being softer and more suitable for a children's toy.

# 3D Printing Process 

### 1. Printer Number
N/A – The machine number was not recorded during the printing process.

### 2. Raft
No raft was used because the print was able to adhere directly to the build plate.

### 3. Wall Thickness
- Wall thickness: 0.86 mm
- Top shell thickness: 1.0 mm
- Bottom shell thickness: 0.6 mm

### 4. Infill
- Infill percentage: 15%
- Infill pattern: Grid

A 15% grid infill was used to provide internal support while keeping the print relatively lightweight and reducing printing time.

### 5. Supports
Supports were used around the screwdriver shaft to prevent the material from sagging during printing and to help maintain the shape of the part.

### 6. Support Removal
The supports were removed by hand. They peeled away from the printed part without requiring additional tools.

### 7. Number of Layers
The print used a total of 112 layers.

### 8. Layer Thickness
The layer height was set to 0.20 mm to provide a balance between print quality and printing time.

### 9. Build Volume
The dimensions of the printed screwdriver were:
- Length: 2.5183 in
- Width: 1.0302 in
- Height: 0.8886 in

### 10. Support Surface Finish
The supports peeled off easily and left a very smooth surface. The only noticeable texture was the typical finish associated with 3D printing.

### 11. Print Layout
The screwdriver was positioned on the build plate to improve the overall print quality and produce a cleaner final part.

### 12. Build Orientation
The screwdriver was printed on its side to help maintain the shape of the part and improve print quality.

### 13. Slicer Settings
The following settings were used in PrusaSlicer:

- Material: PETG
- Infill: 15% Grid
- Wall thickness: 0.86 mm
- Top shell thickness: 1.0 mm
- Bottom shell thickness: 0.6 mm
- Layer height: 0.20 mm
- Supports: Enabled
- Raft: Disabled

PETG was selected because it provides good strength and durability. The remaining settings were chosen to balance print quality, material usage, and printing time.

# Lessons Learned 

Throughout this project, I learned how to take an idea and turn it into a physical product using CAD and 3D printing. One thing I learned was how to use curved 3-point lines in CAD to create the filleted edges of the flathead screwdriver. This helped me improve my CAD modeling skills and understand how to create smoother shapes.

I also learned how material selection, print orientation, and support settings affect the final print.

The design and printing process went smoothly, and I did not run into any major problems. The screwdriver was a simple design, which made it easy to model and print. The final product came out as expected.

If I were to improve the design, I would experiment with different shapes, colors, and handle designs to make it more appealing to children.
